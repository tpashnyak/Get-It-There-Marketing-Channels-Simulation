      Why Do Middlemen Exist?

      A marketing channel consists of the individuals and firms involved in making a product or service available for use or consumption by consumers or industrial users. So why not just sell direct every time?

      Try It: Count the Contacts

      Four kayak makers want to reach four outdoor shoppers. Toggle the view and count the lines.

      Intermediaries cut the number of transactions, so products and services flow through the channel more efficiently. They create value by performing transactional, logistical, and facilitating functions.

      WholesalerAn intermediary who sells to other intermediaries, usually to retailers; usually applies to consumer markets
      RetailerAn intermediary who sells to consumers
      DistributorAn imprecise term for intermediaries who perform a variety of distribution functions, including selling, maintaining inventories, and extending credit; more common in business markets but may also refer to wholesalers

      Many Paths to the Buyer

      Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) Channels

      Consumers buy by interacting with print or electronic media, with no face-to-face meeting with a salesperson. Examples include mail-order, direct mail, catalogs, telemarketing, websites, and televised home shopping.

      Multichannel (Omnichannel) Marketing

      Blends different communication and delivery channels so they reinforce each other in attracting, keeping, and building relationships with customers, both in stores and online. Ethan Allen does this with stores, a website, and catalogs. BOPUS means buy online, pick up in store. BORIS means buy online, return in store.

      Dual Distribution

      A firm reaches different buyers through two or more different types of channels for the same basic product.

      Strategic Channel Alliances

      One firm's marketing channel is used to sell another firm's products. Alliances are popular in global marketing because they're easier and cheaper than building a channel from scratch.

      Marketing Matters: Cereal Partners Worldwide (CPW)

      A strategic alliance built as a global business. General Mills (U.S.) brings cereal manufacturing and marketing. Nestlé (Switzerland) brings worldwide distribution. CPW competes in 130+ markets and was expected to reach about 10% of the world cereal market. That's why Cheerios carries the Nestlé name in many countries.

      Name That Strategy

      ~3 min. A quick discussion prompt: "Is Tidewater selling through its own website and Flint River Outfitters already multichannel?" (Yes, it's the start of one.) Dual distribution is about reaching different buyers through different channels.

      Vertical Marketing Systems

      Vertical marketing systems (VMS) are professionally managed, centrally coordinated marketing channels designed to achieve channel economies and maximum marketing impact.

      Explore the Family Tree (Figure 12-6)

      Select a branch to open it.

      Vertical Marketing Systems
      Forward integration: a producer owns the intermediary at the next level down (for example, a manufacturer that owns its own retail stores).
      Backward integration: a retailer owns a manufacturing operation.
      Wholesaler-sponsored voluntary chainsThe wholesaler coordinates buying, merchandising, and inventory for independent retailers. Ex: Ben Franklin
      Retailer-sponsored cooperativesIndependent retailers form an organization to run a wholesale facility. Ex: Associated Grocers
      FranchisingA parent company and individual firms agree to operate a specific business
      Manufacturer-sponsored retailEx: Ford (car dealers)
      Manufacturer-sponsored wholesaleEx: Pepsi-Cola (soft drink bottlers)
      Service-sponsored retailEx: Holiday Inn (hotels)
      Service-sponsoredEx: H&R Block (tax service)
      An administered VMS achieves coordination at successive stages of production and distribution through the size and influence of one channel member rather than through ownership or contracts.

      Classify the System

      Choose the type of VMS for each example, then check your answers.

      ~4 min. Contractual systems are the most popular. Emphasize the difference between the three: ownership (corporate), contracts (contractual), and influence (administered).

      Choosing the Right Channel

      Marketing executives ask three questions when choosing a channel or intermediary:

      1. CoverageWhich gives the best coverage of the target market?
      2. BuyersWhich will best satisfy buyers' requirements?
      3. ProfitWhich will be the most profitable?

      Channel Conflict & Cooperation

      Channel conflict arises when one channel member believes another channel member is engaged in behavior that prevents it from achieving its goals.

      Vertical conflict happens between different levels of the same channel. One common source is disintermediation, when a channel member bypasses another member and sells or buys products direct.

      Horizontal conflict happens between intermediaries at the same level of the channel.

      In the slide photo, a Goodyear dealer's "authorized Goodyear reseller" sign has authorized crossed out and replaced with antagonized. Goodyear's dealers were angry after the company began selling tires through large discount retailers.

      Supply Chain vs. Marketing Channel

      Logistics: activities focused on getting the right amount of the right products to the right place at the right time at the lowest possible cost. Logistics management organizes the cost-effective flow of goods to satisfy customer requirements and deliver customer service.
      Supply chain: the firms involved in performing the activities required to create and deliver a product or service to ultimate consumers or industrial users. Supply chain management integrates information and logistics across firms to create and deliver products and services that provide value to consumers.

      Responsive or Efficient?

      Aligning a supply chain with marketing strategy takes three steps. Put them in order.

      ⚡ Dell: A Responsive Supply Chain

      Builds customized computer systems and relies on more expensive express transportation. Customers want the most up-to-date system, customized for them, and they're willing to wait for delivery and pay a reasonable price.

      💲 Walmart: An Efficient Supply Chain

      A reliable, lower-price retailer. It uses cross-docking: products from suppliers go straight from inbound trucks to outbound trucks bound for stores, with little or no time spent in storage. Customers expect reliable, lower-priced items.

      Crack the Bullwhip

      The bullwhip effect is the tendency for supply chain managers at different levels to exaggerate the need to increase or decrease inventory in response to variation, or lack of predictability, in customer demand.

      Bullwhip Simulator

      Shoppers buy a few more kayaks in week 4. See how each level of the supply chain reacts.

      ConsumersRetailer ordersWholesaler ordersManufacturer production

      Remember the empty shelves of 2020? Signs read, "Some products may be temporarily unavailable due to high demand." A demand spike set off panic ordering up the chain, which is the bullwhip effect in real life.
      ~3 min. Slide the over-reaction to 0% (no bullwhip) and then to 150%. Then turn on data sharing. Connect this to the Warm Up: Gulf Coast doubling its order "to be safe" is exactly how a bullwhip starts. The 1.5-minute video can open the stop or be assigned beforehand.

      Closing the Loop: Reverse Logistics

      Reverse logistics is the process of reclaiming recyclable and reusable materials, returns, and reworks from the point of consumption or use for repair, remanufacturing, redistribution, or disposal. It benefits the environment, reduces waste, and lowers operating costs.

      Making Responsible Decisions: HP Recycles e-Waste

      0megatons of electronics go to landfills each year worldwide
      0territories served by HP recycling
      ~3 billionpounds of hardware recycled

      HP collects used computer and printer hardware and uses recycled plastic to make new products. Hundreds of millions of TVs, monitors, and cell phones are thrown away every year. UPS is another big player: it runs returns and repair logistics for other companies.

      Forward or Reverse?
